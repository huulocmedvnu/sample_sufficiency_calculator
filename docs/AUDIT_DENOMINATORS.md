# Audit: denominators of the sufficiency-regime spectrum

**Status:** findings only. No manuscript text, fixture, script or figure is changed by this document.
No fixes are proposed. This is a record for the author to decide on.

**Scope:** how the over / under / ghost / detectable percentages (Table 1, Table 4, Figure 3, Figure 4)
are counted — specifically, *which conditions each percentage is computed over*. Four independent
denominator problems were found. None of them is declared in the manuscript.

**Repo state:** the manuscript is at its pre-session (author) state — EmeraldBay spectrum on the `N>=300`
basis, `4.6 / 84.0 / 11.4`, and no cross-screen table. A same-session commit (`cd59bdd`) that had moved
EmeraldBay to `N>=400` and added a "Table 5" was **reverted** before this audit was committed; see the
Provenance note below for why. The "current number" fields under each finding are these restored author
values. Evidence figures were computed from the committed fixtures and from the per-condition summary
`eb_work/out/pseudobulk.npz` (per-condition sufficient statistics; **not** a re-stream).

---

## Provenance: the `300 -> 400` change was a wrong-direction patch (made, then reverted)

For the record, so it is not repeated. During the session that produced this audit, commit `cd59bdd`:

- changed the EmeraldBay spectrum threshold from `N>=300` to `N>=400` (`4.6/84.0/11.4 -> 4.5/82.9/12.6`),
  updating Table 1, Figure 4b, the L305 detection-floor sentence, and the HANDOFF; and
- added a cross-screen "Table 5" placing all six screens (including Tahoe's `10.6%` next to EmeraldBay's
  `4.5%`) in one column, plus a golden test locking those values.

It was reverted in full because **`N>=400` does not fix the root cause**:

1. `N>=400` is still a *held-out* eligibility threshold ("room to subsample"), not a spectrum rule — the
   same class of error as `N>=300` (Finding 4). It merely swaps one undeclared cell-count filter for another.
2. It still sits on `per_group_slope`, which is pooled by **drug name** (wrong counting unit, Finding 1),
   so the EmeraldBay `4.5%` is still ~3x the natural-unit value (`~1.4%`).
3. Committing it made the manuscript *look* re-validated when the underlying denominators were unchanged;
   and a Table 5 putting `10.6%` beside `4.5%` under one column would have **legitimized the very
   inconsistency this audit documents**.

Lesson: the fix for Findings 1–4 is not another cell-count threshold. Do not re-apply a `300 -> 400`
(or any `N>=k`) patch to the spectrum denominator.

## EmeraldBay design (from the official dataset metadata + `eb_work/out/sample2cond.json`)

EmeraldBay = 1.8M cells, **52 cell lines**, **91 drug treatments** (with combinations). The local
`sample2cond.json` has **96 samples**, each a *treatment* = a `(drug, dose)` pair (or a drug combination),
e.g. `smp_804 -> [('Cetuximab', 0.0686 uM)]`, `smp_805 -> [('Encorafenib', 0.1 uM)]`. The 96 samples are
**~91 drug treatments + 2 vehicle controls** (`DMSO_T0`, `DMSO_TF`), including **16 combination** treatments
(e.g. `Gemcitabine+Paclitaxel`, `Fluorouracil+Leucovorin+Oxaliplatin`). **Dose is carried by the sample**:
36 of the 37 distinct drugs appear at ≥2 doses (= ≥2 samples). Therefore the natural per-measurement unit
**(sample × line) = (drug × dose × line)** — the same unit as Tahoe's "drug × dose × line", and the correct
basis for a like-for-like EmeraldBay-vs-Tahoe comparison.

## Finding 1 — EmeraldBay's spectrum pools over dose and is mislabeled "condition × line"

**What.** The EmeraldBay over/under/ghost in Table 1 (and the Figure 4b bar) is generated by
`scripts/make_unified_calibration_table.py::emeraldbay_row()`, which reads
`fixtures/emeraldbay_falsification_full.json -> per_group_slope`. That fixture groups by
**(drug name × line)**:

- its `cond` field is a **drug name** (`Adagrasib`, `Capecitabine`, `Cetuximab`, `DMSO_T0`, …) — **27
  distinct drug names**, i.e. **dose has been stripped and pooled** (there are 93 distinct (drug, dose)
  treatments but only 27 drug-name groups);
- 27 drugs × 48 lines = **1,064 groups**; each group is a single line (its `line` field is one CVCL name).

So one Table 1 row is built at **(drug × line), doses pooled** granularity but is **labelled
"condition × line"** and placed next to the Tahoe row labelled "drug × dose × line". The unit is therefore
**coarser than Tahoe's** — the dose axis is collapsed.

**Not pooled across lines (a hypothesis that was checked and refuted).** The largest group is
`Adagrasib × SW480 = 40,029 cells`. That is within a single line: SW480 (CVCL_0546) has **313,881 cells**
in total (`pseudobulk.npz` per-line sum), so a 40,029-cell (drug × line) group fits inside one line and does
**not** require pooling across the 52 lines. The coarsening is over **dose (and replicate wells)**, not over
cell line.

**Why it matters — pooling doses mechanically inflates %OVER.** A condition is OVER when `N0 >= n*`, and
`n*` depends only on the effect magnitude and the platform noise, **not** on how many cells were pooled.
Collapsing a drug's doses into one (drug × line) group makes `N0` large (up to 40,029) while leaving `n*`
essentially unchanged, so pooled groups cross the `N0 >= n*` line far more often than the individual
(drug × dose × line) measurements do.

**Evidence (magnitude of the effect):**

| unit / filter | %OVER |
|---|---|
| **(drug × line), doses pooled, N>=300** — the committed Table 1 value | **4.6%** |
| (drug × line), doses pooled, N>=400 — the reverted patch | 4.5% |
| **(drug × dose × line), unfiltered (N>=1)** — the natural unit, matching Tahoe (`pseudobulk.npz`) | **~1.4%** |

The reported ~4.6% is ~3× the natural-unit value. (That gap combines *both* the dose-pooling and the
cell-count filter of Finding 2; the two act in the same direction. The `pseudobulk` figures use the
diagonal-covariance approximation the manuscript itself states it uses, so treat the EmeraldBay absolute
numbers as indicative — the ghost fraction in particular does not reproduce the committed value, see
Finding 4 note.)

**Where affected:** Table 1 (EmeraldBay row), Figure 4b (EmeraldBay bar), the L305 detection-floor sentence
(EmeraldBay median m), and any prose comparing EmeraldBay's over-sampled fraction to Tahoe's.

**Current number in the manuscript:** EmeraldBay `4.6% over / 84.0% under / 11.4% ghost` (N>=300).

**Severity: HIGH.** The EmeraldBay over-sampled fraction is placed directly beside Tahoe's 10.6% as a
cross-atlas comparison, but the two rows are computed at different granularities (pooled drug vs
drug×dose×line) *and* different cell-count filters, so the comparison is not like-for-like.

## Finding 2 — three different, undeclared cell-count denominators for the same quantity

**What.** The over/under/ghost "sufficiency spectrum" is reported for three modalities using three
different implicit thresholds on acquired cells, none of which is stated in the manuscript:

| dataset | spectrum denominator (implicit) | source |
|---|---|---|
| Tahoe | **N0 >= 1** (no filter) | `fixtures/tahoe_quota_per_condition.csv`, min N0 = 1 |
| EmeraldBay | **N >= 300** | `per_group_slope`, `min_cells_slope = 300` |
| Orion / TRADE | **N >= 25** | `--min-cells 25` in the recompute passes |

**Why it matters — the Tahoe 10.6% is a diluted estimate.** Because low-cell conditions have a noisy,
near-zero magnitude (mostly classified UNDER), *including* them lowers %OVER. Sweeping the Tahoe threshold
(exact, from the committed CSV):

| N0 threshold | conditions | over | under | ghost |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| **>=1 (committed headline)** | 56,827 | **10.6** | 89.0 | 0.4 |
| >=25 | 54,953 | 11.0 | 88.6 | 0.4 |
| >=100 | 52,742 | 11.4 | 88.1 | 0.5 |
| >=400 | 48,146 | **12.4** | 87.1 | 0.5 |

The headline **10.6%** is computed over a denominator that includes **8,681 conditions with N0 < 400**,
of which **1,478 have fewer than 20 cells** — a regime the manuscript's own Discussion calls uninterpretable
(the TRADE `EIF4A3` example: "an artifact of only seven cells"). Restricted to conditions where the
magnitude is estimable, the over-sampled fraction is ~11–12%, i.e. the reported 10.6% is a slight
*under*-estimate, not an over-estimate.

EmeraldBay moves the same way (indicative, `pseudobulk`, sample × line): %OVER ~1.4 at N>=1 rising to ~4.5
at N>=400.

**Where affected:** the headline **10.6%** (Abstract, Figure 3, Table 1, Results, Summary, Discussion), the
EmeraldBay spectrum (Table 1, Figure 4b), and the genetic detectable/over/under denominators (Table 4)
which are on the N>=25 scored set.

**Current numbers:** Tahoe `10.6 / 89.0 / 0.4` (N0>=1); EmeraldBay `4.6 / 84.0 / 11.4` (N>=300);
genetic percentages over the N>=25 scored set.

**Severity: HIGH.** The single most-cited number of the paper (10.6%) rests on an undeclared, dataset-
specific denominator rule, and the datasets shown side by side use three different rules.

## Finding 3 — EmeraldBay group count 4,992 counts 80 empty combinations

**What.** Methods (and `docs/FALSIFICATION.md`) state EmeraldBay has **4,992 condition-line groups**.
That number is the **theoretical product 52 lines × 96 samples = 4,992**, not an observed count: the
official design is **52 lines × (91 drug treatments + vehicle controls) = 96 samples**, and `pseudobulk.npz`
contains **4,912** non-empty (sample × line) groups — i.e. **80 of the 4,992 sample-line combinations have
zero cells**. `4,912` is the count of groups that actually contain data.

`docs/DEVLOG.md` (phase 48) records changing `4,912 -> 4,992` in the manuscript, calling `4,912` a "digit
transposition"; it is not a transposition (`4912 -> 4992` changes the third digit), and `4,912` was the
correct non-empty count.

**Where affected:** Methods paragraph on EmeraldBay (the "4,992 condition-line groups" clause),
`docs/FALSIFICATION.md`.

**Current number:** 4,992 (overstates the non-empty group count by 80).

**Severity: LOW.** It is a descriptive group-count, not a denominator of any reported percentage, but it
overstates the real data by 80 empty combinations.

## Finding 4 — the N>=300 threshold has no statistical basis (fixture-reuse artifact)

**What.** The EmeraldBay spectrum denominator is `N >= 300`. That threshold is `MIN_CELLS_SLOPE = 300` in
`scripts/emeraldbay_recompute/pass4_falsification.py` (introduced in commit `c4118da`), commented
**"EXP1/2 group threshold (room to subsample)"** — i.e. the minimum cells the *held-out slope test* needs
to subsample down a geometric grid to `n <= N/2`. It is:

- **not** a spectrum choice (the spectrum subsamples nothing);
- **absent from every doc and the manuscript** (the only "300" in the manuscript is the unrelated
  `R = 300` subsampling-*replicate* count);
- inconsistent with the manuscript's stated thresholds (held-out `N>=400`, gating `N>=100`).

It reached the Table 1 spectrum only because `make_unified_calibration_table.py` reused the slope-test
fixture `per_group_slope` (the only committed fixture carrying per-group EmeraldBay magnitudes). Note that
`N>=400` would be no better (it is also a held-out threshold; see Provenance).

**Note (reproducibility limitation).** The committed EmeraldBay spectrum cannot be reproduced from
`pseudobulk.npz` by filtering: `per_group_slope` is at **(drug × line)** granularity (Finding 1) with a
full sample-covariance `n*`, whereas `pseudobulk.npz` is at **(sample × line)** granularity with the
diagonal approximation. At `N>=400` the two agree on %OVER (both 4.5) but disagree sharply on ghost
(committed 12.6% vs pseudobulk 0.6%) and group count (958 vs 1,260). A clean "unfiltered spectrum" would
require first fixing the counting unit and the covariance method.

**Where affected:** Table 1 and Figure 4b (the EmeraldBay spectrum denominator).

**Current number:** N>=300.

**Severity: MEDIUM.** A subsampling threshold is used as a reporting denominator, and it silently sets the
denominator of a headline comparison.

## Cross-reference: the 6,018 vs 5,503 gap (checked, not a denominator finding)

For completeness, the ~515-condition gap between the spectrum's OVER count (6,018 matched-vehicle
conditions) and the downsampling test's 5,503 was checked and is **not** primarily a low-cell exclusion:
only **47** of the 6,018 OVER conditions have N0 < 400 (and 0 have N0 < 100). The gap is dominated by the
two tests using different baselines (spectrum = matched vehicle; gating = per-line mean) and universes, so
the two counts are not in a subset relationship. The broader "counted in the spectrum but excluded from
validation" concern is real but lives at the dataset level (the spectrum's 56,827 conditions include 8,681
with N0 < 400 that the N>=400 held-out excludes), not in the 6,018/5,503 pair.
